# Crewtrace Marketing Plan Slide Copy

## Slide 1: Cover

**Title**
Turning payroll leakage into our sales wedge

**Subtitle**
My California-first plan to get Crewtrace in front of contractors who are still fighting messy field time and payroll.

**Proof point**
$2,100/mo recovered at S&W Waterproofing

---

## Slide 2: Where We Are Right Now

**Title**
What has actually worked so far?

**Body**
So far, the only real customer came through an industry relationship.

That customer paid the $1,000 onboarding fee and now pays $100/month. Everything else we have tried has either created little response or no conversions.

I do not think that means the product is not valuable. I think it means the way we get in front of contractors matters a lot. The sale probably needs trust, context, and a real conversation.

**Supporting points**
- Cold email has not worked.
- LinkedIn has created some conversations, but no conversions.
- Warm industry relationships produced the only sale.
- The strongest signal is that this needs to be sold through trust, not just traffic.

---

## Slide 3: Who We Are Going After First

**Title**
I want to start with California contractors who already feel payroll pain.

**Body**
The best first customer is not every contractor. I want to focus on companies where the problem is big enough to be obvious: hourly field employees, multiple jobsites, and some kind of messy process around time tracking or payroll.

California is a good starting point because labor costs are high, payroll mistakes are expensive, and contractors tend to care a lot about having cleaner records.

**Ideal first segment**
California contractors with 10-75 hourly field employees across multiple active jobsites.

**Who fits**
- General contractors with employees
- Waterproofing, roofing, concrete, HVAC, electrical, plumbing, and landscaping
- Companies using paper timesheets, spreadsheets, or basic clock apps
- Owners who suspect they are losing money but do not have proof yet

**Positioning note**
I do not want to sell legal advice or make compliance claims. The angle is simpler: cleaner time records before payroll closes.

---

## Slide 4: How I Want To Position Crewtrace

**Title**
I do not want to sell "time tracking software." I want to sell payroll proof.

**Body**
If I describe Crewtrace as construction time tracking software, it sounds like every other app in the category.

The sharper message is that Crewtrace helps contractors stop paying for hours they cannot verify.

That is the pain the owner actually feels. They are not waking up thinking they need software. They are wondering why payroll feels messy, why hours keep changing, why the office is chasing down time, and whether they are overpaying without knowing it.

**Recommended message**
Crewtrace helps contractors stop payroll leakage with GPS-verified time records, jobsite geofencing, and payroll-ready exports.

**Short version**
Stop paying for hours you cannot verify.

---

## Slide 5: The Offer

**Title**
I think onboarding should be the thing we sell first.

**Body**
The product is useful, but the real value shows up when we help a contractor set it up correctly and find the payroll problem.

So instead of treating onboarding like a setup fee we have to apologize for, I want to package it as the first product: a Payroll Leakage Recovery Sprint.

**Offer**
Payroll Leakage Recovery Sprint

**What we do**
In 14 days, we review their current time process, set up their active jobsites, onboard their crew, and help them see whether unverified hours are costing them money before the next payroll run.

**Included**
- Time-process audit
- Jobsite and geofence setup
- Crew onboarding
- Payroll-review checkpoint
- Owner-facing leakage summary

**Pricing I want to test**
- $1,000 onboarding
- $100/month for smaller crews
- $150/month for mid-size crews
- $250/month for larger crews

**Important**
I do not want onboarding to be optional. It is how we create the outcome.

---

## Slide 6: Channel Strategy

**Title**
The goal is conversations, not traffic.

**Body**
For the next month, I do not want to measure success by impressions or clicks. I want to measure whether we can get real conversations with contractor owners and turn those conversations into paid onboarding.

The channel mix should be built around that.

**Priority channels**

**Jason-led calls**
Jason, you are better suited for owner conversations because you run a construction company and can talk peer-to-peer. I think this should be our main sales motion.

**Warm intros**
We should use his waterproofing company and our paying GC customer as proof points, then ask for introductions to owners with similar payroll issues.

**Bookkeepers and QuickBooks partners**
These people see payroll mess all the time. If they send us contractors with messy field time, we can run the audit and pay a referral fee when someone becomes a customer.

**Google Search**
Keep this focused on high-intent construction time tracking, GPS time clock, geofence time clock, and QuickBooks-related searches.

## Slide 7: Sales Motion

**Title**
Jason should carry the first conversation.

**Body**
This should not feel like a software pitch from someone outside the industry. It should feel like one contractor talking to another contractor about a payroll problem we have already dealt with.

**Jason's role**
- Make owner calls
- Ask for warm intros
- Tell the waterproofing story
- Talk through payroll pain in plain construction terms
- Close the Recovery Sprint

**My role**
- Build the target-account list
- Keep the ads and landing pages clean
- Prep call notes and follow-up materials
- Handle demo and product questions
- Support implementation after the sale

**Call opener**
"I run a construction company too. We built Crewtrace because paper time and edited punches were costing us money. Crewtrace helped my company find about $2,100/month in payroll leakage. Would it be worth a 10-minute look at whether the same thing is happening in your payroll?"

---

## Slide 8: Paid Search

**Title**
I want to keep Search narrow and stop overvaluing cheap clicks.

**Body**
The ad data is still early, so I do not want to overreact. But the pattern is clear enough: Demand Gen can create a lot of cheap traffic without proving that the traffic is qualified.

Search is more expensive, but it is closer to actual buying intent. That is where I want to focus.

**What we have seen so far**
- Demand Gen: 687 clicks, 0 conversions
- Search: 39 clicks, 0 conversions
- Total reviewed ad spend: about $294

**What I want to do**
- Keep Search tightly focused on construction-specific terms
- Send traffic into the payroll leakage and calculator funnel
- Pause or cap Demand Gen unless it becomes retargeting
- Measure calculator completions and booked-call clicks, not impressions

**Core terms**
construction time tracking, construction time clock app, GPS time clock app, geofence time clock, contractor time tracking app, QuickBooks construction time tracking

---

## Slide 9: 30-Day Plan

**Title**
My goal is three paid Recovery Sprints in 30 days.

**Body**
If we can get three contractors to pay for onboarding in the next month, I think that is a real signal. It means the offer is understandable, the problem is painful enough, and this sales motion can work.

**Week 1**
I'll package the Recovery Sprint, clean up the website copy, and build a list of 150 California contractors.

**Week 2**
Jason, you start owner calls, ask for warm intros, and begin outreach to bookkeepers, QuickBooks partners, and other referral partners.

**Week 3**
We run the first audits, demo Crewtrace around each contractor's actual payroll problem, and start turning what we learn into stronger trade-specific proof.

**Week 4**
We close paid Sprints, review what worked, and tighten the playbook for the next month.

**Targets**
- 150 target accounts
- 20 real owner conversations
- 8 audits booked
- 3 paid Recovery Sprints sold

**Bottom line**
If this works, we double down on owner conversations, referral partners, and narrow Search. If it does not, we should know exactly which part broke: the list, the pitch, the offer, or the funnel.
